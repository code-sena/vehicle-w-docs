# Discovery Brief — Vehicle Washing

## 1. Research objective

The objective of this discovery phase is to validate whether the problems identified in the product framing actually affect the target users of Vehicle Washing.

The research focuses on understanding how customers and car wash staff currently manage:

- Customer registration and access.
- Vehicle registration and management.
- Service selection.
- Price consultation.
- Appointment scheduling.
- Availability management.
- Service execution and status tracking.
- Promotions.
- Payments.
- Service history.

The research will also identify which problems have the greatest impact and which proposed product capabilities provide the highest value.

---

## 2. Research questions

The research should answer the following questions:

1. How do customers currently request a car wash service?
2. How do customers find available services and prices?
3. How do customers schedule an appointment?
4. What difficulties do customers experience when scheduling?
5. How do customers manage information about their vehicles?
6. How do customers learn about promotions?
7. How are payments currently handled?
8. How do customers know the status of their service?
9. How do customers access their service history?
10. How do car wash operators currently manage appointments?
11. How do operators manage service availability?
12. How are vehicles and customers registered?
13. How are operators assigned to services?
14. How are service statuses updated?
15. How are promotions managed?
16. How are payments recorded?
17. What are the most frequent operational problems?
18. Which parts of the current process consume the most time?

---

## 3. Target participants

The research should include people who directly participate in the car wash service process.

### Customers / vehicle owners

People who own or regularly use a vehicle and have experience requesting car wash services.

### Car wash operators

Workers responsible for receiving customers, managing appointments, performing services or updating service status.

### Administrators

People responsible for managing the establishment, services, users, promotions, payments or operational information.

---

## 4. Research methods

The initial discovery phase will use qualitative research methods.

### Interviews

Semi-structured interviews will be conducted with customers, operators and administrators.

The interviews should focus on actual experiences rather than hypothetical opinions.

For example:

> "Tell me about the last time you scheduled a car wash."

is preferable to:

> "Would you use an application for scheduling car washes?"

The first question provides evidence about current behavior, while the second mainly captures an intention or opinion.

### Observation

When possible, the current process should be observed directly in a car wash establishment.

The observation should focus on:

- How appointments are received.
- How availability is checked.
- How customer information is recorded.
- How vehicles are identified.
- How services are assigned.
- How service status is tracked.
- How payments are recorded.
- How information is communicated to customers.

### Process analysis

The current workflow should be documented to identify:

- Manual steps.
- Repeated data entry.
- Information gaps.
- Delays.
- Communication problems.
- Possible sources of errors.

# 10. Expected patterns

The research should look for recurring patterns rather than isolated opinions.

Examples of relevant patterns include:

### Pattern: Appointment friction

Multiple customers report difficulties finding or confirming available time slots.

### Pattern: Information fragmentation

Customers or operators need to use multiple channels to complete a single process.

### Pattern: Manual data management

Operators repeatedly enter or update customer, vehicle or appointment information manually.

### Pattern: Lack of service visibility

Customers have limited information about the status of their appointment or service.

### Pattern: Promotion uncertainty

Customers or operators have difficulty determining which promotions are valid and how they should be applied.

These are **examples of patterns to investigate**, not confirmed findings.

## 4. Métodos de investigación

La fase inicial utilizará métodos de investigación cualitativa.

### Entrevistas

Se realizarán entrevistas semiestructuradas a clientes, operadores y administradores.

Las entrevistas deben enfocarse en experiencias reales y no únicamente en opiniones hipotéticas.

Por ejemplo, es preferible preguntar:

> "Cuéntame sobre la última vez que programaste un lavado."

en lugar de:

> "¿Usarías una aplicación para programar lavados?"

La primera pregunta proporciona evidencia sobre el comportamiento actual, mientras que la segunda principalmente recoge una intención u opinión.

### Observación

Cuando sea posible, se observará directamente el proceso actual en un establecimiento de lavado.

La observación debe enfocarse en:

- Cómo se reciben las citas.

- Cómo se comunica la información al cliente.

### Análisis del proceso

El flujo actual debe documentarse para identificar:

- Pasos manuales.
- Registro repetido de información.
- Falta de información.
- Retrasos.
- Problemas de comunicación.
- Posibles fuentes de error.
